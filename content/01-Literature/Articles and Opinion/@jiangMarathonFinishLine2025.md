> Abstract
            Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly evolving and increasingly being integrated into health care. Medical students and residents must develop a foundational understanding of AI’s applications and implications for their education and training, professional practice, and the health of their patients. In this commentary, the author, a current MD/PhD student, calls for the inclusion of AI in educational programming and suggests approaches to 5 inherent tensions in efforts to develop AI curricula. Curricula should not fixate on any one tool or technology; however, a core AI curriculum should provide all students and residents with the needed AI literacy and skills, enabling graduates to engage with, question, and responsibly use AI tools in their clinical practice. Learners also should be offered opportunities that prepare them for a variety of roles ranging from users to developers of AI models. Institutional differences in AI resources and expertise pose a challenge, underscoring the importance of unifying national modules and co-production of curriculum with interested learners who have AI experience. Finally, all AI curricula must promote among developers and users an awareness of ethical principles when contextualizing and evaluating AI models.

# Notes
>Jiang notes: five tensions in AI in med-ed:
>(1)  the rapid evolution of AI and its implications for foundational learning in medicine, 
>(2) curricular  compression and content expansion, 
>(3) uniform programming and diverse learner  backgrounds and goals, 
>(4) institutional capacity and curricular necessity, and 
>(5) technological advancement and ethical accountability.

core curriculum may cover AI tenets, such  as data science principles, machine and deep learning architectures, and LLMs. Learners should  know how to assess standard performance metrics used in publications, develop a critical lens  for questioning model outputs, and recognize embedded biases and hallucinations. Coursework  in these domains, much more so than independent study, has been indispensable to help me  better understand the latest AI literature and move past a “black box” view of model outputs.
