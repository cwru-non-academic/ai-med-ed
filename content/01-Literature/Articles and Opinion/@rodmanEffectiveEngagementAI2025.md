> Abstract
There is broad agreement that the cognitive abilities of generative artificial intelligence (GAI) technologies are existential opportunities for medical education, threatening to reshape both the clinical practice environment and the nature of learning. Recent improvements in the abilities of these models—most notably so-called reasoning models, which can perform many cognitive tasks with equal or better performance compared to humans—outpaces the abilities of both medical schools, professional societies, and consensus organizations to adapt. In this commentary, the authors propose that this fact should compel individual clinician-educators to become early adopters and actively engage with GAI technologies, with their learners, patients, and educational communities, and provide practical strategies for engagement.

# Notes

## Key Findings
### Reasons to engage
1) GAI helps educators develop clinical assessments, prepare papers, code etc
2) Rapid uptake of AI models as scribes, writing tools, consultants, among docs and pts
3) Learners are now saturated in the tech

### Potential risks
1) use of thinking technologies "can cause cognitive deskilling" and
2) early use of tools among early learners "might stunt cognitive development"
3) need to separate tasks that should be delegated (and not emphasized in medical training any more)

### Actions
Prepare adaptive learners who can navigate changing world
Remember old values - empathy, communication, critical thinking, assessing evidence.
